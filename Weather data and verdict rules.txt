Hourly forecast opens the telemetry-style table for the selected observing

Seeing and atmospheric transparency are separate 3-hour forecasts. Seeing
keeps the existing 1–8 categories and arcsecond labels. Transparency categories
1–8 mean <0.3, 0.3–0.4, 0.4–0.5, 0.5–0.6, 0.6–0.7, 0.7–0.85, 0.85–1, >1

The worst available category in the dark window is reported. The provider's
coverage and freshness limits are retained; transparency is never invented
from cloud cover. Seeing/transparency and weather come from different models
and resolutions and can disagree. Missing values stay unfilled.

Sky baseline: saved Sky brightness (Bortle) / SQM settings. SQM is not inferred
from Bortle or tonight's forecast; a missing value remains Not set.

WINDOW AND AGGREGATION
Astronomy Engine computes precise Sun-centre crossings of -18 degrees for
astronomical darkness, -12 for nautical twilight and -6 for civil twilight.
Sunrise/set use the library's standard rise/set event model. Solar darkness
sampling throughout NightLens now uses geometric solar altitude, consistently
with these twilight definitions. The target window still uses 10-minute
visibility samples, so it can start a few minutes after the precise dark time.

The weather summary and hourly table use hourly model values whose hour blocks
overlap the dark window. Edge-hour values can originate before dark start or
before dark end. Cloud layers and temperature/pressure are available-hour
means; wind/gust/rain chance/humidity are peaks. Dew-point spread is the minimum
paired air minus dew-point temperature. Pressure trend is last minus first
available pressure in the window; humidity trend is last minus first divided
by elapsed hours. Wind direction is at the highest available wind speed.
Precipitation is summed over overlapping model hours, not prorated for partial
edge hours. No missing hours are interpolated or silently replaced with zero.

VERDICT: GENERAL PLANNING HEURISTIC
For the night: mean total cloud, peak rain probability, peak wind/gust,
minimum dew spread and peak humidity determine the weather grade.
For each hourly row: the same rules are applied to that row's values.

GO: clouds <=20%, rain chance <25%, wind <20 km/h, gusts <30 km/h,
dew spread >2 C, humidity <=85%, with no known caution condition.
MARGINAL: clouds >20% through 70%, rain chance >=25% but <50%, wind >=20
but <35 km/h, gusts >=30 but <50 km/h, dew spread <=2 C, or humidity >85%.
NO-GO: mean cloud >70%, rain chance >=50%, wind >=35 km/h or gusts >=50 km/h.
Known NO-GO conditions take precedence over missing values. Otherwise, if fewer
than 80% of overlapping rows have cloud, rain probability, wind and paired
air/dew temperature, the night shows LIMITED DATA. Empty windows show NO DATA;
no astronomical darkness shows NO DARK SKY.

The night summary downgrades an otherwise GO weather grade to MARGINAL for
known poor seeing/transparency (category >=6). Hourly status is weather-only;
seeing/transparency are explicitly displayed in the Conditions view. These are
fixed starting planning limits, not equipment wind ratings or promises of
image quality. The full hourly table lets users see changes hidden by a mean
or peak. Cloud color thresholds remain <=20% / <=50% / >50%, independent of
the broader GO/MARGINAL/NO-GO classification, and always accompany numbers.

Dew risk: spread <=2 C High, >2 through 5 C Medium, >5 C Low.
Fog potential is Possible when spread <=1 C and humidity >=95%; otherwise Low
when paired data are available. Frost potential is an air-temperature proxy:
Possible when air <=0 C and spread <=2 C. These are estimates, not measured
fog, ground frost or surface condensation. Unknown data stay Not forecast.

MOON AND DARKNESS
Illumination/phase are evaluated at the middle of sampled darkness. Moonrise/
set are events within the selected noon-to-noon observing period. Moon-free
intervals use 10-minute Moon-altitude samples within astronomical darkness,
so boundaries are approximate. Terrain, trees and detailed local refraction
are not modeled. A Moon-free interval does not guarantee clear weather.
All times are local to the selected observing location, across midnight and
DST changes. Repeated hourly clock times show the distinct UTC offsets.

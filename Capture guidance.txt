NIGHTLENS 5.0 — CAPTURE GUIDANCE

USING THE NEW GUIDANCE
Each object card shows a difficulty marker, site Bortle estimate, starting
sub-exposure, gain guidance and total accepted integration goal. Click any card
for Capture plan, Notes & limits, and Sources tabs. All numbers are starting guidance,
not a calibrated optimum or a guarantee of image quality / signal-to-noise.

LIGHT POLLUTION
Gear > Sky brightness (Bortle) opens the location-specific setting and a button
to LightPollutionMap.app at the current coordinates. The initial Centennial
sample is 39.5807, -104.8772: Bortle 8.9, SQM 17.88, shown by that site's UI on
October 24, 2026 as an October 2026 estimate. It is a baseline model value,
separate from the site's moonlight-adjusted night simulation. It is not an
on-site reading. It is initially marked as an area estimate within 5 km of
Centennial center, not a precise measurement at your telescope.

The site's data endpoint rejected direct desktop requests. NightLens therefore
does not claim automatic map synchronization. Open the map, verify your actual
observing site, and enter/confirm its Bortle and optional SQM. Confirmed entries
apply within 0.5 km. Moving elsewhere with Windows Location will not reuse an
out-of-range sample. Multiple site samples are retained. Bortle 0 means unset;
unknown sky brightness does not produce an invented total-time goal.

SQM is recorded as source context, not used as a calibrated sensor-noise model.
Bortle is the input for the approximate plan tiers described below. Moonlight,
target altitude, haze, filter bandwidth, target surface brightness, sensor QE,
read noise and full well also matter. A single Bortle class cannot specify the
exact exposure and gain. Calibrated tools such as SharpCap's measured sky / sensor
analysis can refine the plan.

BEGINNER MARKER
The star plus 'Beginner-friendly' is an equipment/sky heuristic, not a universal
catalog designation. Bright, sufficiently large clusters and selected accessible
targets can qualify when they fit one frame and are at least 40 degrees high.
In bright urban skies, the mark is more restrictive for faint broadband targets.
The Moon uses lunar-mode guidance. Unmarked objects may be more demanding or
simply not rated by this heuristic. A star never promises success in clouds.

EXPOSURE AND GAIN LIMITS
Seestar S50 is recognized separately from S50 Pro. Official Seestar support
documents 10, 20 and 30 second deep-sky choices. NightLens uses only those
choices for the S50. Its Alt-Az starting plan uses 10 seconds; EQ plans
may use 20/30 for appropriate targets, sky brightness and filters. Return to
10 seconds if tracking/rejected frames or clipping require it. The S50's gain
is app-controlled, never a manual recommendation. Lunar/planet modes use
their own short/auto controls instead of the deep-sky sub-exposure list.

Choose Smart telescope in Equipment, then select a Seestar model or Other.
Select the optical channel beside Shoot with on the dashboard when shown. Enter only the exposure durations your current
telescope app actually offers. Original S50 accepts only 10/20/30 s. Pro model
profiles allow confirmed additional durations up to 60 s; the full set of app
choices is not inferred from a maximum. Conservative defaults are 10 s, except
the unverified original S30 wide channel, which needs manual specifications.
The planner chooses from that list; an empty list produces a request for those
capabilities, not a guessed exposure. Gain remains app-controlled. The app does
not infer another model's limits from Seestar S50 or S50 Pro limits.

For ASI585MC Pro, the starting gain is 200, following ZWO's current HCG product
specification as checked October 24, 2026. Older materials cite 252: verify your
installed driver/firmware. Gain is not increased automatically with Bortle.
Avoid clipping bright stars/cores; inspect test frames and use shorter subs or
an appropriate lower-gain setting if the driver/calibration calls for it.
Unknown cameras get a request to use a calibrated camera preset.

The conventional-camera sub-exposure heuristic starts at 90/60/30/15 seconds
for Bortle <=3 / <=5 / <=7 / >7. It scales by a bounded focal-ratio factor and
can increase for filtered emission targets. Bright clusters and Orion's bright
core limit the starting duration. Conventional tracked subs are capped at 180s,
Alt-Az starts are capped at 10s, and an untracked tripod gets a <=1s test-frame
suggestion. An unspecified mount must be configured. These are cautious test
plans, not calculations proving the sky background dominates read noise.

TOTAL ACCEPTED INTEGRATION
The ranges are practical initial goals. They do not represent a requested
signal-to-noise threshold and cannot predict image quality:
  Bright cluster: 0.3–0.8 h reference goal
  Orion bright-core target: 0.5–1.5 h
  Reflection/dark nebula: 3–6 h
  Other emission / planetary nebula / SNR: 1.5–3 h
  Other broadband target: 2–4 h

A bounded focal-ratio factor (f/5 reference, 0.5–3x) scales these goals. For
broadband non-cluster targets the Bortle <=3 / <=5 / <=7 / >7 factors are
1 / 1.5 / 2.5 / 4. For clusters the factor is 1 + max(0,Bortle-4)*0.15;
filtered emission targets use 1 + max(0,Bortle-4)*0.16. Ranges are rounded up
to quarter hours for presentation. They may be accumulated over several nights.
They count retained exposure only: rejected frames, dithering, slews, download
time and overhead do not count. Moon/planet capture uses short video guidance,
not hours of deep-sky exposure. Bright Moon and a poor match between a dark/dust
target and an urban site are flagged in Notes. A darker site may be preferable
to extending a broadband session indefinitely.

DEDUPLICATION AND GROUPED REGIONS
Explicit catalog aliases now normalize case, spacing and leading zeros and are
joined across selected files. The Eastern Veil's NGC6992 and NGC6995 entries are
also consolidated into one imaging-region card, preserving both identifiers.
These are distinct catalog components, not literally the same catalog object.
The grouped region uses a conservative approximate 70 x 25 arcminute footprint;
verify exact framing. Western Veil remains a separate recommendation. Nearby
unrelated targets are not merged just because they are close together.

ZWO LIBRARY AND MODEL-SPECIFIC GAIN (VERSION 6)
The local library contains 91 camera entries and nine Seestar optical profiles.
Thirty camera entries have documented numeric HCG starting guidance. Unknown
values stay explicitly unverified; camera users can save their calibrated gain
override per equipment setup. Seestar gain is always app-controlled. ASI Air
and Duo profiles use the imaging sensor and allow camera gain controls.

HCG thresholds are not unity gain and are not automatically scaled by Bortle.
Color/mono, Pro/uncooled and read-mode variants can differ. Existing ASI585MC
Pro setups map to gain 200; earlier 252 documentation is called out in notes.
ASI678MC uses its manual's 182 threshold; the mono variant stays unverified.
Model source URLs and review dates are in EquipmentLibrary/zwo-profiles.json.
Specialist ASI990/991/992 SWIR profiles withhold visible-light Bortle-based
exposure, integration and beginner guidance.

Changing a selected model clears the previous model's gain override. Custom
sensor dimensions remain editable; missing dimensions block saving until set.
The original S30 wide camera is offered explicitly with missing specifications,
rather than silently borrowing the tele camera's sensor. The library is local
and dated, with no automatic online update or camera-control connection.

SETUP TYPE AND LENS (VERSION 6.1)
Camera and Mount are generic labels. Custom setup supports any maker through
Camera > Other, manual model/specifications and an optional calibrated gain.
Smart telescope > Other uses a separate specifications dialog and app exposure
choices. All smart-telescope gain remains app-controlled.
The dashboard lens selector appears only for a selected multi-lens Seestar.
Changing lens replaces optics/sensor dimensions and recalculates target framing
and capture guidance. Lens choice persists. Original S30 Wide requires verified
manual sensor dimensions before its first plan; cancelling retains the old lens.
